🔍 What is State PSC?

Every Indian state has its own Public Service Commission (PSC) that recruits officers for state-level administrative, police, and other government services. Examples: MPPSC (MP), UPPSC (UP), MPSC (Maharashtra), TNPSC (Tamil Nadu), BPSC (Bihar), CGPSC (Chhattisgarh). These are equivalent to UPSC but at the state level.

📋 Exam Structure

01 Prelims Objective — GS + CSAT (Screening stage)
02 Mains Descriptive papers — GS + Optional Subject
03 Interview Personality Test — Final selection

Note: Each state's PSC pattern varies slightly. Always check the official notification for your state's specific syllabus and pattern.

✅ Eligibility (General)

  • Any Bachelor's degree from a recognized university
  • Age: 21–40 years (varies by state and category)
  • Domicile / residence certificate of that state required
  • Knowledge of the state's official language (for some posts)

📚 How to Prepare

Common Subjects (All State PSCs)

  • History — Indian + State-specific history
  • Geography — India + State geography
  • Polity — Constitution, Panchayati Raj, local governance
  • Economy — Indian economy + state budget
  • Current Affairs — national + state-level news
  • State-specific GK — art, culture, tribal areas, schemes

Resources

  • NCERT Books (foundation for all PSCs)
  • State government official portal — schemes and policies
  • State PSC previous year papers — most important
  • Drishti IAS, Vision IAS materials (adaptable for state exams)

🎖️ Posts After State PSC

  • Deputy Collector / SDM — Sub Divisional Magistrate
  • DSP — Deputy Superintendent of Police
  • Block Development Officer (BDO)
  • Tehsildar / Naib Tehsildar
  • Commercial Tax Officer
  • Treasury Officer
  • Jail Superintendent, Labour Officer

💰 Salary & Perks

Deputy Collector ₹56,100 Pay Level 10
DSP ₹44,900 Pay Level 7
Perks Bungalow Vehicle, Staff
Pension Lifetime + Medical Benefits

⚖️ Pros & Cons

👍 Pros

  • Work in your home state — no distant transfers
  • Less competition than UPSC
  • Strong local impact — grassroots level governance
  • High prestige at district and state level

👎 Cons

  • Still highly competitive — lakhs apply
  • State-specific syllabus requires extra preparation
  • Political interference can be high in some states
  • Promotion ceiling lower than IAS/IPS officers
