🔍 What is a Corporate Counsel?

A Corporate Counsel (In-house Counsel) is a lawyer employed directly by a company to handle all its legal matters. Unlike law firm lawyers, corporate counsels work exclusively for one organization — advising on contracts, employment issues, mergers, regulatory compliance, and litigation strategy.

🧠 Is This Career Right for You?

  • You prefer working within one organization over multiple clients
  • You enjoy the intersection of law and business strategy
  • You want better work-life balance than a law firm offers
  • You are commercially minded and understand business needs
  • You can translate legal complexity into business decisions
  • You are a team player who works with all departments

🎓 Education & Skills

Education

  • LLB / BA LLB mandatory
  • LLM in Corporate, Commercial, or IP Law preferred
  • 2–5 years experience in a law firm first (recommended)
  • CS (Company Secretary) qualification is a bonus

Key Skills

  • Contract negotiation and drafting
  • Corporate governance and compliance
  • M&A and due diligence
  • Employment and labour law
  • Intellectual property management
  • Regulatory and government affairs

💼 Responsibilities

  • Reviewing and drafting all company contracts
  • Advising Board of Directors on legal risks
  • Managing mergers, acquisitions, and joint ventures
  • Ensuring regulatory and statutory compliance
  • Handling employment disputes and HR legal issues
  • Overseeing external law firms hired for litigation

💰 Salary Range (India)

  • Junior In-house Counsel: ₹8 – ₹15 LPA
  • Senior Counsel: ₹20 – ₹40 LPA
  • Legal Head / VP Legal: ₹50 – ₹1 Cr LPA
  • General Counsel (MNCs): ₹1 Cr – ₹3 Cr+

⚖️ Pros & Cons

👍 Pros

  • Better work-life balance than law firms
  • High salary, especially in MNCs and tech companies
  • Deep involvement in business strategy and decisions
  • Job security with company benefits — bonus, ESOPs

👎 Cons

  • Limited courtroom experience over time
  • Career tied to company performance
  • Can feel isolated as the sole legal voice
  • Business pressure can conflict with legal judgment
