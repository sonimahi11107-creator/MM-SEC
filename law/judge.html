🔍 What is a Judge?

A Judge presides over court proceedings, interprets the law, evaluates evidence, and delivers verdicts. Judges ensure that legal proceedings are fair and conducted according to the law. In India, judges serve in District Courts, High Courts, and the Supreme Court — each with increasing authority and responsibility.

🧠 Is This Career Right for You?

  • You have exceptional knowledge of law and legal procedures
  • You are completely impartial and unbiased in decision-making
  • You have high ethical standards and personal integrity
  • You are analytical and can evaluate complex evidence
  • You are patient and composed under pressure
  • You are deeply committed to justice and the rule of law

🎓 How to Become a Judge

Path 1 — Judicial Services (Lower Courts)

  • Complete LLB degree
  • Pass State Judicial Services Exam (conducted by High Courts)
  • Minimum 3 years of advocacy experience (some states)
  • Selected as Civil Judge / Judicial Magistrate

Path 2 — High Court & Supreme Court

  • Minimum 10 years as an advocate in High Court
  • Recommended by collegium of senior judges
  • Appointed by President of India

💼 Types of Judges

  • Civil Judge / Judicial Magistrate (entry level)
  • Additional District Judge
  • District & Sessions Judge
  • High Court Judge
  • Supreme Court Judge
  • Chief Justice of India (highest position)

💰 Salary (India)

  • Civil Judge (entry): ₹77,840/month
  • District Judge: ₹1,44,840/month
  • High Court Judge: ₹2,50,000/month
  • Supreme Court Judge: ₹2,50,000/month + perks
  • Free housing, car, medical, and pension benefits

⚖️ Pros & Cons

👍 Pros

  • Highest respected position in the legal system
  • Complete job security — judges cannot be dismissed easily
  • Excellent government benefits and post-retirement perks
  • Direct impact on justice and society

👎 Cons

  • Very long path — takes 15–20+ years to reach High Court
  • Enormous workload — Indian courts have millions of pending cases
  • No freedom to practice privately once appointed
  • Under intense public and media scrutiny
